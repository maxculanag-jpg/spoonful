Glossary of cooking terms

Here is my glossary you can use to learn along the way darling. <3

Knife techniques

  • Chiffonade shif-oh-NADStack leafy greens or herbs, roll them up, and slice into thin ribbons.
  • DiceCut into small, even cubes.
  • Julienne zhoo-lee-ENCut into thin matchstick-sized strips.
  • MinceChop very finely.
  • Brunoise BROON-wahzVery fine dice (about 1/8 inch) cut from julienned vegetables.
  • ChopCut into small, slightly irregular pieces.
  • CubeCut into even cubes, about 1/2 inch on each side.
  • MatchstickCut into thin strips about 1/8 inch thick.
  • PareTrim away a thin layer of skin with a paring knife or peeler.
  • PeelRemove the outer skin or rind of a fruit or vegetable.
  • ScoreMake shallow cuts on the surface to tenderize or decorate.
  • ShaveSlice a very thin layer, such as chocolate or cheese.
  • ShredCut into narrow strips with a grater, or pull apart with forks.
  • SliceCut into thin, flat pieces.
  • SliverCut into long, thin strips.
  • SnipCut into small pieces with scissors.

All terms

  • Al dente al-DEN-tayPasta cooked until tender but still with a slight bite in the centre.
  • Bain-marie bayn mah-REEA gentle water bath — a bowl set over simmering water — used to melt chocolate or cook delicate sauces without scorching.
  • BasteTo brush or spoon melted fat, stock or marinade over food as it cooks, for flavour and moisture.
  • Béchamel bay-shah-MELThe classic white mother sauce — milk thickened with a butter-and-flour roux. Base for Mornay and soufflé.
  • BlanchBriefly boil food, then plunge into ice water to stop cooking and set colour.
  • Bouquet garni boo-KAY gar-NEEA bundle of herbs (usually thyme, bay leaf and parsley) tied together and simmered in soups and sauces.
  • BraiseBrown meat or veg in fat, then cook slowly, covered, in a little liquid until tender.
  • BroilCook directly under or over a strong heat source.
  • CaramelizeHeat sugar until it melts and turns golden, or cook onions low and slow until sweet and brown.
  • ClarifyRemove milk solids from butter (or impurities from stock) to leave a clear liquid.
  • Confit kon-FEESlowly cook food in fat at a low temperature until meltingly tender.
  • Coulis koo-LEEA smooth, strained purée of fruit or vegetables used as a sauce.
  • CreamBeat butter and sugar (or similar) until smooth, pale and fluffy.
  • DeglazeAdd liquid to a hot pan to loosen the browned bits (fond), forming the base of a sauce.
  • Demi-glace duh-mee-GLAHSA rich, reduced brown sauce made from Espagnole and stock.
  • DredgeCoat food in a dry ingredient such as flour or breadcrumbs before frying.
  • EmulsifyForce two liquids that don't mix (like oil and water) into a creamy, stable blend.
  • Espagnole es-pan-YOLThe brown mother sauce — veal stock, vegetables and tomato purée, the base of demi-glace.
  • Flambé flahm-BAYPour warm liquor over food and ignite.
  • FoldGently combine a light mixture into a heavier one with a spatula, keeping the air in.
  • Fond fahnThe caramelized browned bits left in the pan after searing — the flavour base for sauces.
  • GarnishDecorate a finished dish, often with herbs, fruit or a drizzle.
  • GlazeCoat food with a thin, glossy layer.
  • GrateShred food into fine pieces using a grater.
  • Hollandaise hol-un-DAYZAn emulsion of egg yolks, butter and lemon — rich, buttery and lightly tangy.
  • KneadWork dough by hand to develop gluten and make it smooth and elastic.
  • MarinateSoak food in a seasoned liquid to add flavour and tenderness.
  • Mirepoix meer-PWAHA flavour base of diced onion, carrot and celery.
  • Mornay mor-NAYBéchamel with cheese added — a daughter sauce.
  • PoachCook gently in liquid just below boiling point.
  • Purée pyoo-RAYBlend or mash food until smooth and thick.
  • ReduceSimmer a liquid to evaporate water, concentrating flavour and thickness.
  • RenderMelt solid fat slowly over low heat, removing any solids.
  • RoastCook uncovered in a hot oven until browned outside and tender within.
  • Roux ROOEqual parts butter and flour cooked together, used to thicken sauces.
  • Sauté soh-TAYCook quickly in a small amount of hot fat, tossing the food.
  • SearBrown meat quickly over high heat to seal in juices.
  • SimmerCook just below boiling, with small bubbles gently breaking the surface.
  • SteamCook on a rack above boiling liquid in a covered pan.
  • SweatCook vegetables gently in a little fat, covered, until soft and juicy without browning.
  • TemperSlowly raise the temperature of eggs or dairy by adding hot liquid a little at a time.
  • Tomate toh-MAHTThe French mother sauce of tomatoes, onion, garlic and herbs.
  • Velouté veh-loo-TAYStock thickened with a light blond roux — silky and versatile.
  • WhiskBeat ingredients with a wire whisk to combine and add air.
  • ZestThe coloured outer peel of citrus fruit, finely grated.
  • Aioli ay-OH-leeA garlic-and-oil emulsion; popularly a garlicky mayonnaise.
  • Amandine ah-mahn-deenDishes garnished with almonds, usually slivered or sliced.
  • Beurre blanc burr BLAHNKA delicate sauce of white wine, vinegar and shallots emulsified with butter.
  • Beurre manié burr man-YAYUncooked butter-and-flour paste whisked into soups or sauces to thicken and gloss.
  • Beurre noisette burr nwah-ZHETBrown butter — nutty, golden, deeply flavoured.
  • Bisque biskA smooth, creamy soup, classically from shellfish.
  • Blanquette blahn-KETTEA white stew of unbrowned meat (veal or chicken) in a creamy sauce.
  • Bouillon BOOL-yonA clear, seasoned broth; also the cubes or powder used to make it.
  • Canapé can-a-payA bite-sized layered hors d'oeuvre on a sturdy base (toast, cracker, veg).
  • Cartouche kar-TOOSHA parchment paper lid placed on the surface of a simmering dish to slow evaporation.
  • Charcuterie shar-KOO-tuh-reeThe art of preserving meats — sausages, confit, pâté, ham, bacon.
  • Chinois shin-WAHA cone-shaped fine-mesh strainer for silky sauces and purées.
  • Consommé con-so-mayStock clarified with egg whites and concentrated for a clear, strong broth.
  • Court bouillon koor bool-yonA quickly cooked broth used to poach seafood.
  • Crème fraîche krem-freshA thick, lightly tangy fermented cream, richer than sour cream.
  • Croquette kroh-KEHTA breadcrumb-coated patty of meat, fish or veg, fried until crisp.
  • Duxelles dewk-SELLFinely chopped mushrooms sautéed with shallots and herbs, used as a filling.
  • En croute on-KRUTEWrapped in pastry and baked.
  • Entrée ON-trayIn France, the course before the main dish; in the US, the main dish itself.
  • Escalope ESS-kah-lohpA thin, pounded boneless piece of meat, often sautéed or breaded.
  • Fines herbes feenz-erbA mix of parsley, tarragon, chives and chervil.
  • Fondue fon-DUEMelted cheese (or chocolate) served warm as a dip.
  • Fricassée FRICK-a-seeA stew of unbrowned meat braised in a creamy white sauce.
  • Gratiné / au gratin GRA-tee-nay / oh gra-TANBaked with a crust of breadcrumbs and/or cheese.
  • Hors d'oeuvre or-DERVEA small bite served before a meal, often finger food.
  • (au) Jus (oh)-ZHOOServed with its natural pan juices (unthickened).
  • Lardons lahr-DOHNSmall strips of slab bacon, rendered until crisp-tender.
  • (à la) Meunière (ah-la) moo-NYEHRFish dredged in flour, sautéed in butter, finished with brown butter, lemon and parsley.
  • Mise en place MEEZ-ahn-plahsHaving all ingredients prepped and ready before you start cooking.
  • Pâté pah-TAYA seasoned paste of meat, liver or vegetables, served chilled or baked.
  • (en) Papillote (on)-poppy-YOTEFood baked in a parchment or foil packet, steaming in its own juices.
  • Ragoût raa-GOOA hearty, slowly simmered French stew.
  • Soufflé soo-FLAYA puffed baked egg dish with a light, airy texture.
  • Sous vide soo-VEEDCooking vacuum-sealed food in a temperature-controlled water bath.
  • Terrine tuh-REENA pâté cooked in a loaf-shaped mould, usually served chilled and sliced.
  • Vol-au-vent vol-oh-VAHNA light puff-pastry shell filled with a savoury mixture.
