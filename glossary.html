Glossary of cooking terms

Plain-English explanations of the words you'll meet across the recipes. No jargon, no gatekeeping.

  • Al dentePasta cooked until tender but still with a slight bite in the centre.
  • Bain-marieA gentle water bath — a bowl set over simmering water — used to melt chocolate or cook delicate sauces without scorching.
  • BlanchBriefly boil food, then plunge into ice water to stop cooking and set colour.
  • DeglazeAdd liquid to a hot pan to loosen the browned bits (fond) stuck to the bottom, forming the base of a sauce.
  • DiceCut into small, even cubes.
  • EmulsionA stable mixture of two liquids that don't normally combine, like oil and water, held together by whisking (e.g. mayonnaise, hollandaise).
  • FoldGently combine a light mixture into a heavier one using a spatula, keeping as much air as possible.
  • JulienneCut into thin matchstick-sized strips.
  • MinceChop very finely.
  • ReduceSimmer a liquid to evaporate water and concentrate flavour and thickness.
  • RouxEqual parts butter and flour cooked together, used to thicken sauces (white, blond or brown depending on colour).
  • SautéCook quickly in a small amount of hot fat, tossing the food in the pan.
  • SimmerCook just below boiling, with small bubbles gently breaking the surface.
  • TemperSlowly raise the temperature of an egg or dairy mixture by adding hot liquid a little at a time, to avoid curdling.
  • ZestThe coloured outer peel of citrus fruit, finely grated, full of aromatic oils.
